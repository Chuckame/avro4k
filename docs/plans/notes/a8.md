## The finding that reframes the program

**avro4k reads ~2× slower than Apache `SpecificData` in every workload** (2.12/1.97/1.94× on
`complex`, 2.22/1.71/1.49× on `simple`, 1.99/2.17× on `lists`). Write is near parity
(1.08–1.59× slower) and avro4k *wins* `simple` write at 25 and 500 records.

The old "avro4k wins on flat records, loses as nesting grows" story was an artifact of comparing
only against `ReflectData`, which `SpecificData` beats by a flat 2.0–2.6×.

The read ratio is **flat across sizes**, which argues for fixed per-value / per-structure overhead
rather than anything compounding with nesting.

## The methodological finding

**Allocation does not explain the read gap and cannot police it.** avro4k is within 1.2–1.4× of
`SpecificData` on read bytes/op — and allocates *less* on `simple` @500 — while being ~2× slower.
Nullable strings are the clearest case: **−29% throughput, ±0 B/op**.

The mirror image holds on write: avro4k allocates **4.4×** what `SpecificData` does on `complex`
@200 yet runs within 1.08×.

So the plan's "bytes/op is the primary acceptance metric" holds for the **KMP migration** (no escape
analysis on JS/native makes elided allocations real) and for the **write** path, but for decode work
throughput must be judged directly. Recorded in `README.md` as an explicit revision to the plan.
